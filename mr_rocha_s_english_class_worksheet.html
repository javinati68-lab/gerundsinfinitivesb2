Understanding Gerunds and Infinitives

A gerund is a verb form ending in -ing that acts as a noun (e.g., swimming, eating). An infinitive is the base form of a verb preceded by to (e.g., to swim, to eat).

15 Verbs followed by Gerunds:

admit, avoid, consider, delay, deny, discuss, enjoy, finish, imagine, involve, keep, miss, postpone, practice, recommend.

15 Verbs followed by Infinitives:

agree, appear, arrange, ask, decide, demand, expect, fail, hope, intend, learn, manage, offer, plan, pretend.

8 Verbs followed by Both (no meaning change):

begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer, bother.

10 Prepositions followed by Gerunds:

about, after, against, before, by, for, in, instead of, on, without.

Key Vocabulary:

  • Vibrant: Full of energy and enthusiasm.
  • Wander: To walk around slowly in a relaxed way.
  • Gastronomy: The practice or art of choosing, cooking, and eating good food.
  • Excursion: A short journey or trip.
  • Thriving: Prosperous and growing; flourishing.
  • Immerse: Involve oneself deeply in a particular activity or interest.
  • Contemporary: Belonging to or occurring in the present.
  • Unwind: Relax after a period of work or tension.
  • Bustling: Full of energetic and noisy activity.
  • Picturesque: Visually attractive, especially in a quaint or pretty style.
